# Meeting notes for Food System \& Security Topic



\- The city of Vancouver is expecting that around 240,000 people will be added to and around the city by the year 2050



\- They want to map out where that dense population will live over the next 30 years, and have supporting food infrastructure for those people



\- There was a 5 year plan made by the city of Vancouver called 'The Local Food System Action Plan' that was approved in 2021, and now that it's been 5 years they want to plan ahead



\- Basically food has been unevenly distributed in areas like lower-income areas, remote communities, and Indigenous communities



\- So, Vancouver is growing fast in population and neighborhoods are being redesigned while nobody has a clear plan/tool that shows whether Vancouver's food resource is keeping up with the population growth



\- Also, they want to take into consideration whether the recent climate disturbances, such as heatwaves and floods, will also be an issue once these food infrastructures have been implemented



\- This is the reason they want us, BCIT students, to develop an app/tool to help their judgements to deal with this rising concern 


### App Pitch:



An app that simulates how climate disruptions will affect food access across Vancouver neighborhoods, showing city planners where the food system is most fragile and where to invest.



### How would this app calculate how **vulnerable**/**fragile** an area is?:



So to create a calculation, we're taking three things into consideration: climate disturbances, population vulnerability, and food provider diversity.



* For a heat scenario ill give an example. Say we have 2 areas where area A has a lot of outdoor food providers like community gardens, outdoor markets, and food vendors, while area 'B' has a lot of indoor food providers like food banks, community kitchens, and meal programs. We would say that Area A is more susceptible to a flood than Area B so we would consider Area A more **vulnerable**
* For floods, both indoor and outdoor food providers are susceptible to floods when they are within the flood zone
* Each scenario you could take the number of outdoor food providers, divide it by the number of total food providers, and get a result where the higher the number is, the more vulnerable it is to a heat wave
* Also mention (maybe) that earthquakes can affect area's with more indoor providers
* This might not be enough indication for a city planner, so we will also include population into the calculation. Areas with lesser income, more seniors, more renters, and higher population density, are more affected by a climate disruption
* One more thing we should add to the calculation is food provider diversity. An area with both indoor and outdoor food providers are considered more vulnerable in general to areas with only indoor or only outdoor food providers



If we can find a way to quantify each, then we will be able to provide a rating to a city planner who is looking to see which areas are most vulnerable. Essentially, we are doing the thinking for them.
